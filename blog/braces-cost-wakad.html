What Braces Cost in Wakad, and What a Complete Quote Includes

Written by Dr. Hemlata Tanwani, MDS (Orthodontics & Dentofacial Orthopaedics)
Specialist in Orthodontics & Dentofacial Orthopaedics · 15+ Years Clinical Practice

It is the question everyone wants answered first and almost nobody answers on the phone: what will this cost?

There is a reason for that, and it is not evasion.

Orthodontic treatment is not a product with a shelf price. It is eighteen months or so of clinical time, and how much of that time your case needs cannot be known until someone has examined your teeth and looked at a radiograph. Two people walking into the same clinic on the same morning can be quoted very different figures, both of them correct.

What you can do before that appointment is understand what moves the number, and what a quote has to contain before it can be compared with another one. That is what this page is for.

The six things that move the price

1. How much movement your case needs — mild crowding corrected in a year sits at one end; a case needing extractions, space closure and bite correction sits at the other. This is the single largest factor, and it is decided by your teeth, not by the clinic.
2. Which appliance is used — metal braces are the most affordable. Ceramic braces cost more because of the material. Clear aligners usually cost the most, because each case involves digital planning and a laboratory producing a series of custom trays.
3. How long treatment runs — the fee reflects the number of adjustment visits and the chair time behind them. A longer case is a larger number for the same reason a longer job costs more in any skilled trade.
4. The diagnostic records taken — photographs, an OPG radiograph, sometimes a lateral cephalogram or a digital scan. These are what the plan is built on. A clinic with in-house imaging can usually fold this into the treatment fee rather than sending you elsewhere for it.
5. Retainers, and what happens after — treatment is not finished when the braces come off. Retainers are part of the result, not an accessory, and whether they are inside the quoted fee varies considerably between clinics.
6. Who actually treats you — an MDS orthodontist has three years of specialist training after dentistry in exactly this. That expertise is reflected in the fee, and in how predictably complex cases finish.

Notice that five of the six are decided by clinical need rather than by preference. The one you genuinely choose is the appliance, and even there, some cases are only suited to one.

What a complete quote contains

This is the part that makes two figures comparable. A quote that covers everything below is describing the whole treatment. One that does not is describing a starting price.

Item Ask specifically
Diagnostic recordsAre photographs, X-rays and scans included, or billed separately?
All adjustment visitsEvery routine visit for the full duration, or a set number?
Emergency visitsA loose bracket or a poking wire — covered, or charged each time?
RefinementsIf the finish needs extra aligners or extra months, is that included?
RetainersFirst set included? What does a replacement cost later?
Review visits after removalRetention checks in the first year — included or extra?
If treatment runs longDoes the fee change if the case takes longer than estimated?

Why the lowest quote is often not the lowest cost

A figure can be made smaller by moving things out of it. Records billed at the first visit, emergency appointments charged per occurrence, retainers invoiced at the end, and review visits treated as separate consultations all sit outside the headline number while remaining entirely necessary.

Put two quotes side by side with the table above and the gap frequently narrows, sometimes reversing. This is not a reason to assume the cheaper clinic is being deceptive; many price this way as standard. It is a reason to compare the same scope on both sides.

The question worth asking plainly: "Is there anything I will be billed for between today and the day my retainers are fitted?" The answer tells you more than the quoted figure does.

Paying across the treatment

Orthodontics is rarely paid in a single sum. Most practices in Pune spread the fee across the treatment, commonly aligned to adjustment visits, so the cost arrives in instalments alongside the appointments. Worth establishing before you commit: what is due at the start, how the remainder is scheduled, and whether retainers fall inside that schedule or arrive as a separate bill at the end.

Dental treatment is not usually covered by health insurance in India unless it follows an accident, so most patients plan for it directly.

Four questions to take to the consultation

1
What does my case actually need, and why? The reasoning should make sense to you in plain language.
2
What is the total, and what does it cover? Against the table above, item by item.
3
What would change it? Which circumstances would make the final figure differ from today's estimate.
4
Can I have it in writing? A written plan and fee, to read at home and compare properly.

Any practice treating you well will answer all four without hesitation. Our guide on how to choose an orthodontist covers what else to verify before starting, and the braces and aligner comparison explains where the price difference between appliances comes from.
